## The chronology (T1)

- **Civaux-2 construction start April 1991; Olkiluoto-3 construction start August 2005.** IAEA, *Nuclear Power Reactors in the World*, Reference Data Series No. 2, 2023 edition (RDS-2/43), country tables (France FR-73: "1991-4"; Finland FI-5: "2005-8"). The Western European construction-start hiatus is fourteen years by subtraction. OL3 grid connection 2021-12 / commercial operation 2022-3 per the same table; the narrative section notes grid connection in March 2022.
- **EU nuclear generation peaked at 928 438 GWh in 2004; 2024 generation 649 524 GWh (23.3% of EU electricity, 12 member states operating); decline of 29% between 2006 and 2024.** Eurostat, Nuclear energy statistics, Statistics Explained (fetched 2026-08-06).
- Referenda and moratoria (Austria/Zwentendorf 1978, Sweden 1980, Spain 1983, Italy 1987): comparative record in Müller & Thurner (2017), T2, engaged for the political mechanism.

## The German phase-out (T1, read first-hand)

Jarvis, Deschenes & Jha (2022), JEEA 20(3): 1311–1346, author manuscript read in full. Verified from the published tables:

- Table 2: phase-out reduced nuclear output by **57.6 TWh/yr** (81.5 with vs 139.1 without); replaced by hard coal **+16.7**, lignite **+7.6**, gas **+4.9**, oil **+1.2**, net imports **+10.6**, wind and solar **+16.5** TWh/yr.
- Table 4: **+26.2 Mt CO2/yr (+11%)**; excess deaths **+799.8/yr** (6,852.8 vs 6,053.0), dominated by hard coal (+542.7) and lignite (+188.5).
- Abstract: social cost of the phase-out **€3–8 billion per year**, majority from air-pollution mortality.
- The seed's "330–800 deaths, 800 preferred" matches the published table's 799.8 change; the older NBER working-paper abstract said "more than 1,100", superseded by the published version. Use the published numbers only.

## The restart penalty (T1)

- **STUK-B 237** (Finnish National Report, Convention on Nuclear Safety, 8th Review Meeting, July 2019), verbatim: "Construction of the unit started after a long break in nuclear power plant construction in Europe, which had resulted in loss of experienced and qualified engineering and manufacturing resources. Lack of knowledge on Finnish regulatory framework and safety requirements, insufficient completion of the design prior to construction ..." This is the regulator's own causal account and the paper's key non-economic witness.
- **NAO (2026), Sizewell C, HC 33, 20 May 2026**, verbatim: "The French and Finnish projects experienced delays of more than a decade and cost four times their original estimates." The 4x anchor for the model's restart-cost ceiling.
- **NAO (2017), Hinkley Point C, HC 40**: OL3 "expected to cost €3.2 billion and begin generating power in 2009", entry pushed to 2018+ and €5.3 billion over budget as of 2017.
- **Cour des comptes (2025), The EPR Sector, summary**, verbatim: Flamanville 3 "total construction cost estimated at around €23.7 billion in 2023 (including interest during construction)", profitability "below the company's weighted average cost of capital".
- **NEA No. 7530 (2020), Unlocking Reductions in the Construction Costs of Nuclear**: "constructing reactors in pairs reduces the cost of the second reactor by about 15% (NEA, 2000). An additional 5% cost reduction could also be expected for the second pair"; design completion at construction start ranks as a key cost determinant (ETI 2018 data cited there); recommendation to commit to standardized programmes for the series effect. The seed's "15–20% overnight / >60% time-related / 25–40% total" triplet could not be located in 7530 and is not used.

## The cost-escalation literature (T1)

- **Grubler (2010)**, Energy Policy 38(9): 5174–5188: French programme costs escalated despite standardization ("negative learning"); data provenance later contested.
- **Escobar Rangel & Lévêque (2015)**, EEEP 4(2): reanalysis with EDF accounting data; escalation real but milder, and standardization payoffs visible within palier series.
- **Lovering, Yip & Nordhaus (2016)**, Energy Policy 91: 371–382: seven-country construction-cost record; no single universal escalation curve; **Koomey, Hultman & Grubler (2017)**, Energy Policy 102: 640–643, reply on selection and interest-during-construction. Cited as a pair; the paper uses only the weak claim both sides accept: cost outcomes vary strongly by regime and period.
- **Berthélemy & Escobar Rangel (2015)**, Energy Policy 82: 118–130: lead-time and standardization econometrics across French and US fleets; the closest direct test of the continuity mechanism.

## The mechanism literature (T1)

- **Arrow (1962)**, RES 29(3): 155–173: learning by doing.
- **Argote & Epple (1990)**, Science 247(4945): 920–924: learning curves in manufacturing, and knowledge depreciation as one of the explanations for cross-plant variation.
- **Benkard (2000)**, AER 90(4): 1034–1054: organizational forgetting estimated on Lockheed L-1011 production; experience depreciates, so interrupted production raises unit labor requirements. Microfoundation for the model's δ.
- **Blanchard & Summers (1986)**, NBER Macroeconomics Annual 1: 15–78: hysteresis as history-dependence of equilibria (European unemployment).
- **Dixit (1992)**, JEP 6(1): 107–132: investment hysteresis under sunk costs and uncertainty; the band of inaction. Both hysteresis sources are cross-domain and are cited as the concept's origin, with the transplant stated as the paper's own move.
- **Davis (2012)**, JEP 26(1): 49–66: sober baseline on nuclear economics; guard against triumphalism.

## The 2022 stress test (T1)

- **IEA (2022), A 10-Point Plan to Reduce the European Union's Reliance on Russian Natural Gas**: 2021 imports from Russia ~140 bcm pipeline + ~15 bcm LNG = 155 bcm, "around 45% of the EU's gas imports in 2021 and almost 40% of its total gas consumption."
- **RTE, Electricity Report 2022, key findings** (fetched 2026-08-06): French nuclear fleet availability **54%** in 2022 vs **73%** average 2015–2019; output **279 TWh**, down **81.7 TWh** from 360.7 in 2021; France a net importer (16.5 TWh) "for the first time since 1980"; causes: stress-corrosion cracking found late 2021 plus COVID-postponed maintenance. This is the anti-triumphalist exhibit: the year that exposed the gas dependence also exposed a concentrated same-design fleet.
- **Euratom Supply Agency, Annual Report 2022**: "The situation relating to fuel for Russian-designed VVER reactors, for which no alternative fuel had been licensed, was the most acute." Residual nuclear-side dependence; forbids autarky claims.
- **IPCC AR6 WGIII SPM (2022), B.4.1**, verbatim: "From 2010 to 2019, there have been sustained decreases in the unit costs of solar energy (85%), wind energy (55%), and lithium-ion batteries (85%)". Forbids the nuclear-instead-of-renewables counterfactual.
